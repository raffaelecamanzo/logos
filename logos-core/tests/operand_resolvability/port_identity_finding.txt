THE TWO READINGS OF "THIRD-PARTY", AND WHY BOTH ARE PRINTED
------------------------------------------------------------

This was found during the run and is reported rather than resolved by fiat.

The floor declaration says: "THIRD-PARTY means, exactly: consumer member !=
provider member" -- while citing, as its whole motivation, S-384's split of 11
self-ties and one third-party edge. Those two statements are inconsistent.
S-384's self-ties are NOT consumers bound to themselves; its finding says so
directly:

    "11 of the 12 are ties the CONSUMER creates against ITSELF: an aggregator
     re-registers the callee's template on its own controller, so two members
     serve it, path-only refuses, and identity binds. ... identity's genuine
     THIRD-PARTY contribution, WHERE THE TIE IS BETWEEN TWO OTHER MEMBERS, is
     ONE edge."

So S-384's discriminator is the SERVING SET, not the two member names. Under the
declaration's literal wording, S-384's own headline would split 12 third-party /
0 self-tie, contradicting the sentence that justifies the word.

The declaration is committed evidence and is NOT revised after the run. Both
readings are therefore measured, the verdict is read off the declared one, and
the run ASSERTS that the S-384 reading is below the floor too. A future gate
declaring this metric should write S-384's definition, not this one.

THE 3 NET-NEW EDGES ARE ALL SELF-TIES UNDER S-384'S RULE, and that is the
substantive finding. Every one of them is `mailbox-aggregator-api` calling a
service whose route template `mailbox-aggregator-api` ALSO registers on its own
controller:

    mailbox-aggregator-api -> official-log-export-api  /v1/users/{}/mailboxes/{}/official-log
    mailbox-aggregator-api -> official-log-export-api  /v1/users/{}/mailboxes/{}/official-log/{}
    mailbox-aggregator-api -> reporting-api            /v1/users/{}/mailboxes/{}/usage

In each, exactly 2 members serve the template and one of the two IS the consumer.
The ambiguity the port "resolved" is one the aggregator manufactured by
re-exposing its callee's route, so the port bought no fact the member did not
already hold about itself. This is S-384's mechanism, reproduced verbatim by a
different signal.

The 8 already-bound edges are real cross-service couplings — funnel-aggregator-api
and mailbox-aggregator-api to notification-api, filters-api and pecserver-facade,
and notification-adapter to notification-api — but path-only matching reaches
every one of them alone (exactly 1 member serves each template), so they are not
evidence for identity. They are what FR-WS-04's exactly-one rule already binds.

The nineteenth is worth naming, because an earlier version of this harness could
not see it. `official-log-ingestion-batch` commits

is a good approximation, not a fact: the estate also commits an IP literal and a
`changeit`, neither of which any resolved call site reaches.


THE TWO SAME-TIER COLLISIONS, AND THE FOUR UNMATCHED PORTS
-----------------------------------------------------------

Two members claiming one port resolve to NOTHING, mirroring FR-WS-20 AC2's
same-tier collision rule and NFR-RA-05's prohibition on fabricating an edge.
CR-124's risk table predicted this failure mode and named 9009 and 9007. Both
are real; 9007 is declared twice but never referenced by a resolved call site,
so the two collisions that actually bite are 9009 and one the CR did not
predict:

    9000  ->  NOTHING;  claimed by deprecated-mailbox-core and mailbox-api
                        referenced by funnel-aggregator-api, mailbox-aggregator-api
    9009  ->  NOTHING;  claimed by archive-api and archive-api-logiclens-fork
                        referenced by funnel-aggregator-api, mailbox-aggregator-api

The 9009 collision is the SAME fork repository that cost S-384 its `archive-api`
edges through the host-label ladder. One estate artefact defeats both signals at
the same two members, by two different routes.

Four referenced ports are claimed by no member's `server.port`, ENUMERATED HERE
FOR HUMAN ADJUDICATION rather than absorbed into a count:

    8000  -- `mailserver.api.base-url`, read by archive-manager. A mail server,
             not a workspace member.
    8082  -- `pec-server.base-url`, read by pecserver-facade. The PEC server the
             facade fronts; it is not in the workspace.
    8083  -- `notification-gateway.api.base-url`, read by notification-adapter.
             An external notification gateway.
    9050  -- `pec-manager.api.base-url`, read by mailbox-aggregator-api. No
             member of this estate declares 9050 as its own `server.port`.

None of the four is a workspace member's port. Three are named for systems that
are demonstrably outside the estate, and the fourth is a member-shaped name the
estate does not contain. A signal that guessed a member for any of them would be
fabricating an edge, which NFR-RA-05 forbids — so "resolves to nothing" is the
correct outcome for all four, not a shortfall.

RECONCILIATION AGAINST S-384 — AND WHY THE TWO DO NOT CONTRADICT
-----------------------------------------------------------------

Both rows below are computed in ONE process, from ONE walk of the estate, and
the third-party column of both is computed by S-384's OWN rule, so this is a
reproduction rather than a quotation:

                                            net-new  third-party  self-tie
    S-384  (host label, deploy evidence)         12            1        11
    S-400  (runtime port, application config)     3            0         3

The S-400 run ASSERTS that re-splitting S-384's own pairs reproduces its recorded
1. If it ever stops doing so, either this gate's self-tie rule is not S-384's or
S-384's finding text has drifted from its measurement, and the comparison would
be meaningless; the run fails rather than printing two numbers side by side.

WHAT DIFFERS. S-384 joins the first DNS label of a DEPLOY-overridden base URL to
a member's Service / chart / Compose / directory / application name. S-400 joins
the PORT of the APPLICATION-committed base URL to that member's own
`server.port`. The populations are DISJOINT — `TargetRef::is_deploy` is the
partition — and each signal is silent on the other's rather than negative about
it: every application-committed base URL on this estate has the host label
`localhost`, which no member claims, so S-384's ladder produces nothing there by
construction; and no deploy values file is read here at all.

So the readings are not contradictory, and the difference in the headline figures
(12 against 3) is not evidence that one of them is wrong. CR-124's premise — that
this is new evidence rather than a re-proposal of S-384's — is CONFIRMED. What is
falsified is the hypothesis that the new evidence is favourable.

WHAT THE TWO AGREE ON, and this is the durable finding. In both experiments,
nearly every ambiguity identity discharges is one the consumer created against
itself by re-registering its callee's template on its own controller: 11 of 12
for S-384, 3 of 3 here. Two independent signals, joined on two different bytes of
two different URLs, read out of two disjoint corpora, and both land on the same
wall. The obstacle is not which identity signal is used. It is that on this
estate the exactly-one rule's ambiguity is overwhelmingly self-inflicted, and
resolving it yields an edge from a member to a service it already fronts.


WHAT THIS FINDING DOES NOT SAY
-------------------------------

It does not say the port signal is unreadable. 18 of the 19 base URLs read carry
a port, 5 ports name exactly one member, and the join produces 17 classified
edges (3 net-new, 8 already bound by path alone, 6 whose target serves no such
route). Every mechanical step CR-124 describes works on committed source.

It does not say a port tier would be harmful. It says a port tier would produce
at most 3 net-new edges on the one estate available to measure it against a floor
of 16, and ZERO that a service boundary was crossed to reach — which is not a
basis for extending FR-WS-20's ladder, and certainly not the "new evidence"
FR-WS-22's withdrawal banner requires.

It does not test CR-124's assumption CRA-02 — that `localhost:<port>` generalises
beyond this estate. CR-124 records that assumption as unvalidated and says the
gate cannot test it. It still cannot. One estate cannot establish a convention's
generality, and a falsification on that estate does not need to.

It does not re-derive CR-124's hand census of "39 call sites / 9 service pairs".
That census counted `base-url` values carrying a port against members declaring
it, without the exactly-one rule, without the provider route index, and without
the self-tie split. This run's comparable figures are 19 base-url values read,
6 third-party couplings and 8 already-bound edges. CR-124 said of its own census:
"This census is not a finding ... it must not become an acceptance floor." That
was correct, and the floor was correctly taken from S-384 instead.

Nothing here needs to be re-derived by hand. Re-run before re-deciding:
