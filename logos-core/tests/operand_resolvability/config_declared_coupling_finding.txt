S-411 — IS CONFIG-DECLARED COUPLING THERE TO BE REPORTED?  THE RECORDED FINDING

S-411, measured 2026-09-15 against ~/source/pec-services (84 members, read-only
against the estate SOURCE; the persisted 1.4.11 .logos stores were not read).

  THE PAIR HALF IS FALSIFIED.  THE TOPIC HALF HOLDS.

The two halves come apart, and that is the finding. ADR-65 proposed one intake
carrying two relations. One of them — `declares-topic(A -> T)` — is there in
quantity and clears its floor by 30%. The other — `addresses(A -> B)` — lands ONE
pair below its floor, and does so with a named blind spot in the reader that is
LARGER than the margin.

Floors, declared 2026-09-15T07:08:24Z BEFORE this code was written: >= 12
addressed member pairs and >= 10 shared topics, both taken verbatim from CR-131
section 3.2 B1. The declaration is tracked beside this file in
config_declared_coupling_floor.txt, and the gate PARSES both figures out of it and
compares them to the constants, so a constant cannot be edited to clear a future
run without editing the declaration too.

  THE BLIND SPOT IS BIGGER THAN THE MARGIN, AND THAT IS STATED AT THE HEADLINE

  The declaration fixes the corpus as the SHIPPED `parse_yaml`, deliberately, so
  the admission rule could not be moved after seeing the answer. That parser binds
  no scalar under a YAML SEQUENCE — its own doc comment records why, a `routes:`
  list that once fabricated a `spring.cloud.gateway.routes.uri` key — and the
  estate's structured gateway upstream table is a sequence.

  Measured, not assumed: 84 `host:`-shaped lines sit inside a sequence item in an
  admitted deploy file. Judged by the SAME classifier and at the SAME grain, six
  of those lines would add TWO ordered pairs the headline does not count:

    hermodr-gateway                  -> webmail        (3 overlays)
    mailbox-aggregate-hermodr-gateway -> mailbox-api   (3 overlays)

  11 + 2 = 13 >= 12.  THE BLIND SPOT COVERS THE GAP.

  So the honest statement of this half is not "the estate does not commit enough
  member-grain coupling". It is: UNDER THE CORPUS THE DECLARATION FIXED, the
  estate proves 11, and the distance to the floor is smaller than a limitation of
  the reader that was named before the run and measured during it. The verdict is
  FALSIFIED because the declaration is the contract and its COMMITMENT section
  forbids revising it after a run. What would SETTLE the question is a
  sequence-reading configuration corpus, measured — not this ceiling, which is an
  upper bound that admits every line without judging any of them.

  EVERY captured topic identity on this estate is shared. That is a stronger
  result than the floor asks for and it has one cause: the estate's convention is
  `spring.kafka.topics.<k>: <k>` in every member that touches the topic, so a
  topic that is captured at all is captured at a site whose operand is a
  placeholder every participant commits.

    archive-commands          3    archive-message-queue      4
    archive-volume-counters   2    delayed-output-messages    4
    export-jobs-commands      2    export-jobs-outcomes       2
    export-jobs-requests      2    mailbox-commands           3
    mailbox-reporting         2    notifications              4
    pec-server-commands       3    pec-server-outcomes        3
    unread-mails-requests     2

  TWO OF THE 36 DECLARATIONS DO NOT COME THROUGH `spring.kafka.topics`, and both
  are the case CR-131 section 2.1 calls "config-only publishers":
  `funnel-aggregator-api` names archive-message-queue through `filteraggregate.topic`,
  and `mailbox-unread-mail-batch` names unread-mails-requests through
  `spring.kafka.template.default-topic` — Spring Batch's `KafkaItemWriter`, which
  has no publish call site at all. The relation reaches a producer the invocation
  and broker intakes structurally cannot.

  THE AS-WRITTEN READING ANSWERS 0, EXACTLY AS THE DECLARATION PREDICTED. Under
  FR-WS-10's key rule in force a subscribe is keyed by the placeholder literal as
  written, so the join is `archive-events` against
  `${spring.kafka.topics.archive-events}` — a configuration VALUE against a
  configuration REFERENCE. No member commits the placeholder, so the reading
  cannot return anything but zero. It is printed because the cost of the in-force
  key rule should be visible, and it is not the gate: the declaration fixed
  RESOLVED as decisive before the run, on the ground that a topic's identity is
  the name it carries on the broker.

  WHAT THIS HALF DOES NOT SAY. It does not say the product can see 13 topics
  today. The captured population is 13 literals from 16 `@KafkaListener`
  subscribes and ZERO publishes — the estate's 14 Kafka Streams sites and 54
  header-form publishes are captured as nothing or refused. The relation measured
  here is what committed configuration proves ABOUT those 13; widening the
  captured set is cluster A's work (S-408, S-409, S-410), not this gate's.


  RECONCILIATION AGAINST S-384 AND S-400

    distinct member labels among deploy target references    11   S-384: 11  exact
    distinct external labels among the same references       27   S-384: 28
    members declaring their own `server.port`                30   S-400: 30  exact
    distinct `server.port` values declared                   27   S-400: 27  exact
    distinct ports referenced by an application-committed URL 31
      of which identifying exactly one member                 6   S-400: 5   >=

  The member-label figure and the whole port index reproduce S-384's and S-400's
  exactly, which is what makes this run a measurement of the same estate rather
  than of a different one wearing the same words. Two figures differ, and both
  differences are explained by a population, never by a rule:

  - EXTERNAL LABELS 27 vs S-384's 28. S-384 counted `archive-api` as external
    because the fork collision leaves it claimed by nobody. This run classifies
    that label as a SAME-TIER COLLISION, a bucket S-384's census did not carry, so
    the label moves rather than disappears. 27 + 1 = 28.
  - UNIQUE PORTS 6 vs S-400's 5. S-400 read the 19 base-url values a RESOLVED
    CALL SITE reads; this run reads every application-committed URL value, a wider
    key population over the same source set. S-400's figure is therefore a floor
    for this one, and the index behind both is byte-identical.

  consecutive runs of this harness on one machine, moving with the page cache
  alone. Counts reproduce; times do not.


  WHAT THIS FINDING DECIDES

  ADR-65 is marked SUPERSEDED IN PLACE and never deleted, with this measurement
  and its date, following the procedure ADR-64's identity half followed on S-384.
  NO requirement is created — FR-WS-25 stays proposed in CR-131 section 5.1 and
  unwritten. S-412, S-413, S-414, S-415 and S-418 stay UNPLANNED.

  The topic half's 13 does not on its own plan a story, and the reason is
  structural rather than a judgement call: every one of S-412 through S-415 is
  either the deploy-overlay corpus, the member identity registry, the intake that
  carries BOTH relations, or the surfaces that render both — so none of them is
  scoped to the half that cleared. S-418 rides the `addresses` relation directly
  and is the falsified half. A delivery story scoped to `declares-topic` ALONE
  does not exist today; creating one is a re-decision of CR-131 section 8, on this
  evidence, not a consequence of this run.

  WHAT WOULD CHANGE THIS MEASUREMENT, in the order the evidence ranks them:

  1. A sequence-reading configuration corpus. Two of the 84 sequence host lines
     are pairs the headline misses, which alone clears the floor. This is the one
     change whose effect is already measured rather than argued.
  2. Cluster A's capture. 13 captured topic identities is what 16 subscribes and
     zero publishes yield; S-408/S-409/S-410 widen that population, and the topic
     half would be re-measured over a larger denominator.
  3. Nothing else. The identity classifier, the corpus and the join all worked:
     440 URL-valued and 3 host-valued committed values were read, 75 hit a
     same-tier collision and 336 named an external, and the runnable guard cost
     exactly zero pairs on this estate.

  Re-run before re-deciding: nothing here needs to be re-derived by hand.
