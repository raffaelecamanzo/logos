decide the two change requests. Materiality floor, declared before the run:
>= 5 sites AND >= 10% of the arm's own denominator.

CLIENT CALL (CR-115). 79 of the 140 sites the arm refuses today resolve to a
configuration key on whose value every committed source agrees; 79 of the 88

BROKER PUBLISH (CR-117 §3.3). 38 of 54 message-header publish sites resolve

THE BASE URL, CR-115's other half. 25 `.baseUrl(…)` sites; 21 resolve to a
key, of which 16 agree and 6 disagree. Of the 79 newly-admitted client sites,
51 sit in a unit whose base URL also agrees, 8 in a unit whose base URL does
not, and 20 in a unit that sets no base URL of its own. So the strict reading
— the whole absolute URL must be proven — admits 51, and the FR-WS-08
route-key reading (CR-115 §3.4: "the host need not resolve") admits 79. Both
are material; both are on the record so the CR is decided on the reading it
means.

CONFIGURATION CORPUS. 174 sources (169 .yml, 3 .properties, 2 .yaml), 44
unprofiled and 130 profiled across 5 profiles (docker, it, it-jenkins,
it-local, local), defining 872 distinct keys. 69 @ConfigurationProperties
classes indexed; 13 simple-name collisions resolve to nothing rather than to a
guess. CR-115 CRA-03 — that the sources are committed and discoverable by the
existing FR-SY-11 corpus admission — is confirmed as a by-product.

WHY DISAGREEMENT IS RARE HERE, STATED SO IT IS NOT OVER-READ. The corpus varies
its HOSTS per profile and not its PATHS: `base-url` is redefined in nearly every
test profile, `uri-*` almost never. That is why the path arm shows 2
disagreements and the base-URL arm shows 6 out of a much smaller population.
A corpus that templated its route paths per environment would measure very
differently, and this figure should not be extrapolated to one.
