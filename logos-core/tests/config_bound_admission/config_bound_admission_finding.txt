THE SHORTFALL IS ONE MECHANISM, AND T1 PREDICTED IT
---------------------------------------------------

The members' invocation ledgers hold 132 `http-client-call` rows, of which 44
carry a `${…}` target — the placeholder form the accessor hop records. All 44
reach the payload: nothing is lost in the coverage tier. So the gap is at
capture time, and it is diagnosable site by site.

Diffing the harness's 94-site Java census against those ledgers:

  resolved by the harness AND captured in production      42
  captured in production, refused by the harness           2   (the two
                                                               overlay-divergent
                                                               sites; ADR-64
                                                               admits them,
                                                               CR-115 §3.4 did
                                                               not)
  resolved by the harness, NOT captured in production     37

  All 37 spell `this.`:
      this.mailboxConfigurationApi.getUriGetMailbox()
      this.mailServerConfigurationApi.getUriUpdateArchive()
      this.filterApiProperties.getUriGetFilter()
      …

`extract::config::accessor` refuses a QUALIFIED RECEIVER by design (NFR-RA-05 —
never guess), where the harness's `operand_name` trims the expression to its
last segment and resolves it. S-397 T1's implementation notes record exactly
this ceiling, in as many words: "the census can resolve a site the product will
not". The 79 the criterion was written against is a census figure, and 37 of
those 79 sites are reachable only through that trim.

Arithmetic: 79 agreed − 37 `this.`-qualified = 42, plus the 2 divergent the
product captures = 44.


THE .properties RESIDUE — AC5, STATED AS A NUMERATOR
-----------------------------------------------------

  RESIDUE: accessor sites resolved ONLY by a `.properties` source   0 of 108
  sites a `.properties` source contributes a value to beside a
    source production reads                                         0 of 108
  `.properties` files among the corpus's admitted sources           3 of 174
  `application*.properties` files on the estate's disk                    31
  …of which sit under a hidden `.helm/` directory the discovery
    walk never enters                                                     28

Measured by `configuration_agreement::properties_residue`, whose counterfactual
is production's own: a source whose path ends `.properties` is removed from the
definition set and the site is asked whether any definition survives — which is
what production sees, because production never ingested the file.

A residue of ZERO does not mean the gap is closed. Production ingestion still
reads no `.properties` at all (`source_facts` is reached only for a file the
plugin registry claims; no descriptor claims the extension; no story owns the
artifact plugin that would). What the zero says is what the gap COSTS TODAY on
THIS corpus: nothing, because this estate commits the keys its accessors read
in yaml. It accounts for none of the 44-against-79 shortfall.

This replaces a prose line that read "31 of the 174 discovered sources", which
was wrong on both halves: the 174 admitted sources hold 3 `.properties` files,
and the 31 is a disk count of which 28 are not in the 174 at all.

time: the 1.4.7 stores could not carry it whatever the reading binary. Run once
by hand before the measurement:

recorded here rather than explained, because nothing in this story turns on it.

WHAT A READER SHOULD NOT CONCLUDE
---------------------------------

* Not "the pipeline resolves 44 of the estate's couplings". It admits 44 rows
  carrying a resolved, committed value; 5 of them bind a provider, and none of
  them draws a cross-service edge.
* Not "the .properties gap is closed". It is open, unowned, and currently free.
* Not "79 was wrong". 79 is a correct census figure. What it is not is a
  prediction of the product, because 37 of its sites rest on a receiver trim
  the product refuses on purpose.
